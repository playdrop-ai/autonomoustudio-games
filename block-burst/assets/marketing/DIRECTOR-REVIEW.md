# Block Burst marketing director review

Status: prepared for review. Nothing has been published, uploaded, registered,
funded, or sent externally.

## Recommendation

Approve the campaign with this positioning:

> Drag a piece. Burst a line. Keep the grid alive long enough to chain the next
> combo and beat your best.

The category leaders broadly sell block blasting, daily puzzles, or generic
brain training. Block Burst can own a simpler visible arc: one tactile drag,
one bright burst, then the strategic question of what still fits.

## Creative sequence

1. `DRAG. DROP. BURST.` explains input and payoff together.
2. `PLAN EVERY PIECE` introduces the space-management tension.
3. `CHAIN BIG COMBOS` turns planning into spectacle.
4. `BEAT YOUR BEST` closes on replay value.

Every campaign still uses a countable 8x8 board, a truthful three-piece state,
and protected jewel/plum/gold identity. Video proof is real shipped gameplay
with synchronized SFX. The landscape trailer is one continuous 17-second,
60 fps session on one board. Seven legal moves produce an opening clear, a
second row clear, and a final 3x3 placement that completes six lines and
detonates two bombs. It plays at 1x without cuts, resets, repeated footage, or
retiming.

The AppLovin cut is one continuous 12-second native capture of the same board.
It shows five legal moves: an immediate opening clear, a repair placement, two
compact corner-building moves around one normal tray refresh, then one 3x3
placement that clears six counted lines and detonates two bombs. The move
commits at approximately 9.8 seconds so the full burst and board-clear hold
remain readable.

That approved five-move session powers the shared TikTok, Instagram Reels and
Stories, and YouTube Shorts video at 60 fps. The PlayDrop portrait listing is a
refreshed native take with the same truthful five-move story and the new
color-matched physics debris from `1.0.7`. The PlayDrop landscape listing uses
the same native seven-move session as the YouTube/X trailer without captions.
